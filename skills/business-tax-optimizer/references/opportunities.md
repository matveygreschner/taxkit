# Business tax opportunities under current law (2025-2026)

Researched November 2026 against IRS guidance, statute text and national-firm summaries. Covers P.L. 119-21
("One Big Beautiful Bill Act", OBBBA, July 4, 2025) and Rev. Proc. 2025-32 (2026 inflation adjustments).
Items marked **verify** could not be confirmed in primary sources at research time.

## 1. Cost recovery

**Bonus depreciation, Sec. 168(k).** 100% is permanent for property acquired *and* placed in service after
Jan 19, 2025. Property acquired earlier, including under a binding contract signed before Jan 20, 2025, stays on the
phase-down: 40% if placed in service in 2025, 20% in 2026, 0% after. Elections:
- elect out by class, every year;
- elect 40% (60% for long-production-period property / aircraft) instead of 100%, but only for the first tax year ending after Jan 19, 2025 (in practice, 2025 returns).

Interim guidance is in Notice 2026-11.
*Planning:* document acquisition and binding-contract dates. Consider electing out when you want income, for
example to use expiring NOLs or credits, because of Sec. 163(j) or Sec. 199A interactions, or for state
conformity. Bonus does not reduce AFSI for CAMT.

**Sec. 179.**

| | 2025 | 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Limit | $2,500,000 | $2,560,000 |
| Phase-out begins | $4,000,000 | $4,090,000 |
| Heavy SUV cap | $31,300 | $32,000 |

The deduction is limited to active business taxable income and cannot create a loss; the excess carries forward.
*Planning:* use it for qualified improvement property, roofs, HVAC, fire and security systems, and where a state
conforms to Sec. 179 but not to bonus.

**Qualified production property, Sec. 168(n)** (Notice 2026-16). 100% first-year deduction for the part of
nonresidential real property used as an integral part of manufacturing, production or refining of tangible
personal property.
- Offices, administration, lodging, parking, sales, R&D and software space are excluded.
- Construction must begin after Jan 19, 2025 and before Jan 1, 2029.
- It must be placed in service after July 4, 2025 and before Jan 1, 2031.
- Recapture applies if the production use stops within 10 years (**verify** the period).
- It requires an election.

**Cost segregation.** An engineering study that reclassifies building components to 5-, 7- and 15-year
property, which is now 100% bonus-eligible. A look-back study for property already in service is done through an
automatic Form 3115 with a Sec. 481(a) catch-up. *Risks:* Sec. 1245 recapture on sale; personal property
components aren't like-kind for Sec. 1031; passive and Sec. 461(l) limits.

**Tangible property safe harbors** (unchanged):
- De minimis: $2,500 per item, or $5,000 with an applicable financial statement. Elect annually.
- Small taxpayer building safe harbor: average gross receipts of $10M or less, building basis of $1M or less, and
  annual repairs no more than the lesser of 2% of basis or $10,000.
- Routine maintenance safe harbor.

## 2. Research and development

**Sec. 174A domestic R&E** (Rev. Proc. 2025-28). Deductible when paid or incurred for tax years beginning after
Dec 31, 2024. A taxpayer may instead elect to amortize over 60 months or more. *Foreign* research stays under
Sec. 174 with 15-year amortization.
- **Catch-up, any size:** deduct the remaining unamortized 2022-2024 domestic R&E fully in 2025, or ratably over
  2025-2026. It is made by a statement in lieu of Form 3115. *Two-year electors must take the second half in 2026.*
- **Small business retroactive election:** for average receipts of $31M or less, by amending 2022-2024. **The
  window closed July 6, 2026.**
- The Sec. 174A deduction is reduced by the research credit unless the Sec. 280C(c) reduced-credit election is made.

**Sec. 41 research credit.**
- Regular credit: 20% over a base amount.
- Alternative simplified credit (ASC): 14% of QREs above 50% of the prior 3-year average, or 6% of QREs if any of
  the prior 3 years had none.
- The Sec. 280C(c)(2) reduced credit is multiplied by (1 − 21%), for all taxpayers. It must be elected on a timely
  original return.
- **Payroll offset:** for a qualified small business (gross receipts under $5M and no receipts more than 5 years
  back), up to **$500,000 a year**: $250K against OASDI plus $250K against the employer share of Medicare. Available
  for 5 years. Elect on Form 6765 with a timely original return; claim on Form 8974.
- Eligible small businesses (average receipts of $50M or less, non-public) can use the credit against AMT.
- No OBBBA change.

## 3. Financing and accounting methods

**Sec. 163(j) business interest.** Deductible interest is limited to business interest income + 30% of adjusted
taxable income (ATI) + floor-plan interest.
- **ATI is EBITDA-based again** for tax years beginning after 2024 (permanent).
- From 2026, Subpart F, NCTI and Sec. 78 amounts are excluded from ATI.
- From 2026 the limit applies *before* most interest capitalization, so capitalizing interest no longer avoids it.
  Sec. 263(g) and 263A(f) interest are carved out.
- Exempt if average receipts are no more than $31M (2025) or $32M (2026), unless the business is a tax shelter.
- A real property trade or business can elect out, but must then use ADS, so no bonus.

**Sec. 448(c) gross receipts test: $31M (2025) / $32M (2026)**, 3-year average with Sec. 52 / Sec. 414
aggregation. Qualifying businesses get:
- the cash method (C corps and partnerships with C corp partners);
- no Sec. 471 inventories;
- no UNICAP;
- the completed-contract exemption;
- the Sec. 163(j) exemption.

It is also the Sec. 174A and Sec. 45F small-business test. Method changes are automatic (Rev. Proc. 2025-23).
Syndicates and tax shelters don't qualify.

## 4. Pass-through planning

**Sec. 199A QBI.** 20% deduction, now permanent.
- From 2026 the phase-in range is **$75,000 (other filers) / $150,000 (MFJ)**.
- New **$400 minimum deduction** for at least $1,000 of QBI from businesses in which the taxpayer materially
  participates (2026+, indexed).

| Threshold | 2025 | 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| MFJ | $394,600 | $403,500 |
| MFS | $197,300 | $201,775 |
| Other filers | $197,300 | $201,750 |

*Planning:* tune W-2 wages (this interacts with reasonable comp), aggregate related businesses (Reg. 1.199A-4), keep
income under the threshold with retirement contributions, and use the wider 2026 range for SSTB owners.

**S corp reasonable compensation.** Shareholder-employees must receive reasonable wages before non-wage
distributions, or the IRS can recharacterize distributions as wages.
- Authority: Rev. Rul. 74-44; *Joly* (T.C. Memo 1998-361); *Veterinary Surgical Consultants*, 117 T.C. 141;
  *Joseph M. Grey PA*, 119 T.C. 121; *Watson v. U.S.*, 668 F.3d 1008 (8th Cir. 2012).
- Factors: training, duties, time, dividend history, pay to non-owners, comparable pay.
- *Logic:* distributions avoid 15.3% FICA (2.9% above the wage base), but wages raise the QBI wage limit and are
  not themselves QBI.

**PTET and the SALT cap.** OBBBA **did not restrict** entity-level state tax elections. Notice 2020-75 still
applies.
- Individual SALT cap: $40,000 (2025) and $40,400 (2026), +1% a year through 2029, then $10,000 from 2030.
- The cap phases down by 30% of MAGI over $500,000 / $505,000, but not below $10,000 ($5,000 MFS).
- *Planning:* most valuable for owners at the $10,000 floor (high MAGI) or with state taxes over $40K. It reduces
  QBI. Model state credits for nonresident owners.

## 5. C corporations and losses
- **21% rate:** permanent; unchanged.
- **Corporate charity (2026+):** only gifts above **1% of taxable income** are deductible, up to the 10% cap.
  Amounts lost to the floor carry forward only if the 10% cap is also exceeded. *Planning:* bunch gifts, or use a
  DAF or corporate foundation.
- **CAMT:** 15% of AFSI for corporations with average AFSI over $1B. Bonus and Sec. 174A don't reduce AFSI, so watch
  exposure.
- **Excess business loss, Sec. 461(l)** (non-corporate): permanent. $313,000 / $626,000 MFJ for 2025, then
  **$256,000 / $512,000 for 2026** (reset lower). Excess becomes an NOL. Large bonus or R&E deductions can trip it.
- **NOLs:** the deduction is limited to 80% of taxable income; carry forward indefinitely.

## 6. Benefits and credits

**Retirement plans** (Notice 2025-67 limits):

| | 2025 | 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| 401(k) deferral | $23,500 | $24,500 |
| Annual additions | $70,000 | $72,000 |
| Catch-up, 50+ | $7,500 | $8,000 |
| Catch-up, 60-63 | $11,250 | $11,250 |

Credits for employers with 100 or fewer employees:
- **Sec. 45E startup:** 100% of eligible costs (50% for 51-100 employees), capped at the greater of $500 or the
  lesser of $250 × non-HCEs or $5,000. Lasts 3 years.
- **Employer contribution credit:** up to $1,000 per employee earning $100K or less. 100% in years 1-2, then
  75/50/25%. For 51-100 employees, reduced 2 points per employee over 50.
- **Sec. 45T auto-enrollment:** $500 a year for 3 years.

**Sec. 45F employer childcare (2026+).**
- 40% of qualified childcare expenditures, or **50% for eligible small businesses** (5-year Sec. 448(c) test).
- Cap: $500,000, or **$600,000** for small businesses.
- Resource and referral costs: 10%.
- Intermediary and shared facilities now qualify.
- 2025: 25% / $150,000.

**Sec. 45S paid family and medical leave.** Permanent and expanded for 2026.
- 12.5%-25% of leave wages, up to 12 weeks.
- From 2026 it can be claimed on paid-leave insurance premiums.
- Eligible employees: 6 months of service; part-timers count at 20+ hours a week.
- Leave required by state law doesn't count toward the credit.
- **Verify** the wage cap and policy details against Notice 2026-28.

**WOTC, Sec. 51.** **Expired** for employees beginning work after Dec 31, 2025. Retroactive-extension bills
(H.R. 6231 / S. 3265) were pending as of late 2026. Keep filing Form 8850 within 28 days of hire.

**Energy (closing windows).**
- Sec. 45W commercial clean vehicles: ended for acquisitions after Sept 30, 2025.
- Sec. 45L: no credit for homes acquired after June 30, 2026.
- Sec. 179D: none for property whose construction begins after June 30, 2026.
- Wind/solar under Sec. 45Y/48E: must have begun construction by July 4, 2026, or be placed in service by Dec 31, 2027.
- Other technologies have longer schedules and foreign-entity rules (**verify**).

Flag these only for projects already underway.

## 7. Exit and investment

**QSBS, Sec. 1202** (stock issued after July 4, 2025):
- Exclusion of **50% after 3 years, 75% after 4, 100% after 5**.
- Per-issuer cap of **$15M** or 10× basis (indexed from 2027).
- Gross assets test of **$75M**.
- Non-excluded gain is taxed at 28% (plus NIIT).
- Earlier stock keeps the 5-year / $10M / $50M rules.
- Unchanged: domestic C corp, original issuance, 80% active business test, and the excluded fields (health, law,
  accounting, consulting, financial services, and others).
- AMT treatment of the partial tiers: **verify**.

*Planning:* convert an LLC or S corp to a C corp ahead of a growth or exit path; stack exclusions by gifting shares
to non-grantor trusts.

**Opportunity zones, Sec. 1400Z-2.** Permanent, with rolling 10-year zone designations. New zones start Jan 1, 2027.
For investments after Dec 31, 2026:
- deferral for 5 years (or until disposition);
- **10%** basis step-up at 5 years, or **30%** for rural funds;
- tax-free appreciation after 10 years (FMV basis), with appreciation after 30 years taxable.

Gains deferred under the old regime must be recognized by Dec 31, 2026. *Planning:* gains realized late in 2026
may get more benefit invested on or after Jan 1, 2027 (180-day window).

**Sec. 1031.** Real property only. Unchanged by OBBBA.

## 8. International (not modeled)
- FDII becomes **FDDEI**: Sec. 250 deduction of 33.34%, about a **14%** effective rate (2026+).
- GILTI becomes **NCTI**: 40% deduction, about 12.6%, or about **14%** after the 90% foreign tax credit haircut.
  The QBAI exclusion is removed.
- **BEAT: 10.5%** (2026+).

Refer international structures to a specialist.

## 9. Compliance (2026)
- Form 1099-NEC / MISC threshold: **$2,000** for payments after Dec 31, 2025 (indexed after 2026).
- Form 1099-K: back to **$20,000 and 200 transactions** (retroactive).
- Information return penalty: $340 per return (returns due in 2026).
