# Weight of authority and professional standards

## Primary authority (binding; can be relied on)

| Source | Issued by | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Constitution, Internal Revenue Code (Title 26), tax treaties | Congress / Senate | Code controls; treaties are on par with statutes (later in time generally wins) |
| Treasury Regulations: final > temporary > proposed | Treasury | Final and temporary regs bind; proposed regs are not binding but show IRS position. *Loper Bright* (2024) ended *Chevron* deference; courts now decide the best reading of the statute, giving regs weight based on persuasiveness |
| Revenue Rulings | IRS | Apply law to specific facts; taxpayers with substantially similar facts may rely |
| Revenue Procedures | IRS | Procedures, safe harbors, inflation amounts (e.g. Rev. Proc. 2025-32 for 2026) |
| Notices, announcements in the Internal Revenue Bulletin | IRS | Interim guidance; reliance as stated in the notice |
| Supreme Court decisions | Courts | Binding everywhere |
| Circuit Court of Appeals | Courts | Binding in that circuit (*Golsen* rule: Tax Court follows the circuit to which appeal lies) |
| Tax Court regular decisions > memorandum decisions > summary (S-case) opinions | Courts | S-case opinions have no precedential value and can't be appealed |
| District Court, Court of Federal Claims | Courts | Jury available only in District Court; refund forums (pay first) |

## Secondary authority (explains; not relied on for penalty protection beyond its own weight)
- Private Letter Rulings, Technical Advice Memoranda, Chief Counsel Advice: binding only for the taxpayer
  who requested them. PLRs can count toward "substantial authority" under Reg. 1.6662-4(d)(3)(iii), but can't
  be relied on as precedent.
- IRS publications, form instructions, FAQs, websites: not authority. The IRS isn't bound by its publications.
- Treatises, journals, law reviews, firm alerts, legislative summaries: persuasive only. JCT "Blue Book"
  explanations are given weight as substantial authority.

## Confidence standards (approximate probability of prevailing)

| Standard | ~Likelihood | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Will | ~90%+ | Clear statute or controlling authority on point |
| Should | ~70-75% | Strong authority, minor uncertainty |
| More likely than not | >50% | Required for tax shelter positions; avoids §6694 penalty without disclosure for reportable transactions |
| Substantial authority | ~40% | Avoids §6662 understatement penalty and §6694 preparer penalty without disclosure |
| Reasonable basis | ~20% | Requires disclosure (Form 8275) to avoid penalties; minimum for any return position |
| Not frivolous | >5% | Below reasonable basis; not a supportable return position |

## Professional rules
- **Circular 230** (31 C.F.R. Part 10): due diligence, conflicts, written advice standards (§10.37: reasonable
  factual and legal assumptions, consider all relevant facts, no reliance on audit lottery).
- **IRC §6694**: preparer penalty for unreasonable positions (greater of $1,000 or 50% of fee) and for willful
  or reckless conduct.
- **AICPA Statements on Standards for Tax Services (SSTS)**: return positions, answers to questions, reliance on
  client data, use of estimates, knowledge of errors, form and content of advice.
- **§7525 privilege**: limited federally authorized tax practitioner privilege. Doesn't cover tax shelter
  promotion or criminal matters.
